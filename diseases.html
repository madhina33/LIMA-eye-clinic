Retinal Diseases & Care Guide

Learn about various stages of Diabetic Retinopathy, symptoms, early warnings, and preventive measures.

Stage 1: Mild Non-Proliferative

Mild Diabetic Retinopathy

Microaneurysms start to form, representing small areas of ballooning in the retina's tiny blood vessels.

Symptoms:

Usually asymptomatic in early stages. Vision remains mostly normal.

Precautions:

Strict blood sugar level control, routine annual eye examinations.

Stage 2: Moderate

Moderate Diabetic Retinopathy

As the disease progresses, blood vessels that nourish the retina may swell and begin to block blood flow.

Symptoms:

Occasional blurred vision or minor difficulty reading.

Precautions:

Manage blood pressure, cholesterol levels, and consult an ophthalmologist every 6 months.

Stage 3: Severe

Severe Diabetic Retinopathy

A significant number of blood vessels are blocked, depriving the retina of blood supply, triggering growth signals for new vessels.

Symptoms:

Noticeable dark spots, floating spots (floaters), or fluctuating vision.

Precautions:

Immediate medical intervention, laser treatment evaluation, urgent dietary modifications.

Stage 4: Proliferative (Advanced)

Proliferative Diabetic Retinopathy

Advanced stage where fragile new blood vessels grow along the retina and inside the vitreous gel, leading to severe bleeding risks.

Symptoms:

Severe vision loss, sudden blurred vision, cobwebs in vision field, or complete dark spots.

Precautions:

Immediate surgical procedures (vitrectomy), strict glycemic control, frequent clinical supervision.
